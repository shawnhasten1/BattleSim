# Guide: Build player characters with the character builder

The character builder makes player characters under the 2024 rules (SRD 5.2) or the 2014 rules (SRD 5.1), and you can mix them: a 2024 class with a 2014 race works, and each keeps its own edition's rules. Pick a class, a level, a background and a species, and it works out the scores, hit points, saves, skills, spells and every class and subclass feature up to that level. Hover anything to read its rules. Leveling up adds what the next level gives. This guide makes a wizard, levels a rogue up and into a second class, turns a hand-built PC into a built one, makes a whole party in one click, and adds homebrew content of your own.

> **What you'll end up with:** a Wizard 5, a Rogue 4 / Fighter 1, a rebuilt Barbarian, a party of four, and your homebrew classes and subclasses offered beside the SRD's.

---

## 1. Make a character

Open the **Actors** tab, click **Create Token**, and choose the **Character** tab.

![The Character tab of Create Token](img/build-a-character/01-character-tab.png)

Type a name and choose a **Class**, **Level** and **Background**. A **Species** (a **Race**, for a 2014 one) is optional: without one, set size, speed and senses on the sheet yourself. The **2014 · 2024 · Both** filter chooses which edition's versions the lists show; picking one turns the class, background and species you've chosen into that edition's. The **ⓘ** beside each list shows what the choice gives.

- **Quick build** makes every choice for you (the class's suggested ability scores, skills, feats, spells and equipment) and puts the token on the map.
- **Open the builder…** opens the character builder with every choice filled in, for you to look through and change first. It opens on the same edition filter, and if you were adding to a folder, the character goes in that folder.

> **Either way, nothing is final.** Every choice can be changed later from the sheet.

## 2. The character builder

The builder is one window: the steps on the left, the step in the middle, and on the right a live preview of the sheet the character will have.

![The builder in the Codex look: the Class step, the steps, and the preview](img/build-a-character/02-builder-codex.png)

- **Two looks.** **Standard | Codex**, in the title bar, switches between the app's own look and the Codex sheet's (with its **Dark | Light** colours). PC sheets open in whichever you last chose, and the builder follows yours. Both looks have exactly the same controls.

  ![The same step in the Standard look](img/build-a-character/03-builder-standard.png)

- **Steps, none locked.** **Class**, **Origin**, **Abilities**, **Spells** (for a caster), **Equipment** and **Review**. Each says how many choices are still open there; **✓** means it's done. The header's **N choices left** button goes to the first one. **Next** and **Back** walk through them, but you can go to any step at any time.
- **Hover anything to read its rules.** A class, a species, a background, a feat, a skill, a weapon, a spell, a feature or a number in the preview shows its card after a moment, or when you tab to it. Its dot says whether the simulator runs it: filled, it runs; half, it partly runs (the card says what doesn't); hollow, the DM runs it. **Read all ›** (or the **i** key) pins the full text beside the step.

  ![A spell's rules card](img/build-a-character/04-rules-card.png)

- **The preview** shows the AC, initiative, speed, hit points, ability scores, saves, skills, senses, attacks and features the character will have. A number a choice just changed flashes. Hover a number to see how it adds up.
- **Your draft is kept.** If the window closes by accident, opening the builder for the same character offers **Continue where you left off**. **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) are in the title bar, and its **?** lists the other keys.

### Class

The class's card (hit die, primary ability, saves) opens **The class table, 1–20**. For a new character, **Change class** switches it; your scores, background, species and origin choices stay.

Below it, a panel for each level: the hit points it adds, what got bigger ("Proficiency +3 · 3rd-level slots 2"), each feature with its first line, and that level's choices, inline. A subclass or a feat is a card; skills and expertise are chips showing the bonus each would give; weapon mastery is a table. **✦** marks the builder's suggestion. An open choice offers **Choose for me**. A level's spell choices are one line that goes to **Spells**. **Ahead** lists what the levels to come will bring.

The **Hit points** switch chooses **Average** or **Rolled**: rolled, each level takes a roll you type, or **Roll** rolls one for you.

### Origin

The species (or race) and the background are cards, each saying what it gives in its own edition: a 2024 species' traits, a 2014 race's ability increases. Pick one to see its traits and its choices (a lineage, a size, a skill, an origin feat's spells).

![Origin: species and races, each card saying what it gives](img/build-a-character/05-origin.png)

When the class, species and background aren't all one edition, a note says so: each keeps its own rules.

### Abilities

Scores are placed, not typed. With the **Standard array**, click a value and then a score (or drag it onto the score), and the two swap. **Point buy** has − and + under each score and a meter of the 27 points; each + says what it costs. **Manual** lets you type scores, or **Roll 4d6, drop lowest** and place the rolls. Under each score, its base and every increase on top; ★ marks the class's primary abilities.

![Abilities: the scores as dials, and where the increases come from](img/build-a-character/06-abilities.png)

**Ability increases** says where they come from. A 2024 background gives three points: **+2 and +1** or **+1 to three**. When a 2014 race could give them instead, two cards say what each would give, and you choose. Later increases (an Ability Score Improvement) are set in Class.

### Spells

**Your spells** lists every spell the character has, by level: **P** marks a prepared one, **✓** one always prepared. Click one to open the grid that chose it. One set of filters (search, school, cost, only spells that run, concentration, ritual) serves every grid.

Each spell choice is its own grid. A finished one folds to a line of its picks; **Change** opens it. An open grid shows its picks first, then the rest from the highest level down. Spells the character already has are **tucked away** (with why). A grid of one swaps its spell when you pick another; a fuller grid that's full dims the rest. Finishing a grid opens the next one still open.

![An open grid: the picks first, then by level, the rest tucked away](img/build-a-character/07-spells.png)

Each tile shows the school, what casting it costs (● action, ▲ bonus action, ◆ reaction, ◷ longer), **C** for concentration and **R** for ritual, and its edge is the spell's element colour.

### Equipment and Review

**Equipment** offers the class's and the background's starting packages as cards (a 2014 class's lines, with a table for any weapon it asks you to choose) and lists what reaches the sheet: weapons and armor, each with its card.

**Review** checks that every choice is made and the scores are valid, sums the character up, and **Create character** puts it on the map.

![Review: the checklist and the summary](img/build-a-character/08-review.png)

## 3. Level up

Open the character's sheet. On the **Stats** tab, **Class & level** says what it is and offers **Level up…**, **Level down** and **Open in the builder…**

![Class & level on a built character's sheet](img/build-a-character/09-class-and-level.png)

**Level up…** shows the level turning, what it gives and asks for, and what applying it changes:

![Level up: the class to level, what it gains, its hit points and its choices](img/build-a-character/10-level-up.png)

- **Class to level**: the character's classes, as cards.
- **You gain**: the new features, and what got bigger.
- **Hit points**: the average, or a roll typed or rolled.
- **Choose**: this level's choices, already filled with the builder's suggestions (spells in the same grids as the builder's).
- **What changes**, folded: every record on the sheet the level adds or changes.

Click **Level up to 4**. It's one undo step. Tokens at full health follow the new maximum; a hurt token keeps its hit points.

> **Your edits are kept.** If you changed something the builder made (renamed Cunning Action, typed a different max HP), leveling up leaves it as you made it, says so under **What changes**, and offers to update it to the new level's version.

## 4. Add a second class

**Multiclass…**, beside **Class to level**, adds every other class. A level in a new class is multiclassing. Each card says what multiclassing into it needs: a 13 in the primary ability of the new class and of each class the character has. The window says when the scores fall short, and lets you take the level anyway. The other edition's version of a class the character has can't be taken, and its card says why.

![Multiclass: each class says what it needs](img/build-a-character/11-multiclass.png)

Choose **Fighter** (the Fighter needs Strength *or* Dexterity 13, and the rogue has the Dexterity). Its first level brings Fighting Style, Second Wind and Weapon Mastery, but not the Fighter's saving throws: those come only from the first class. Spell slots from two casting classes are combined by the multiclass rule. A Bard, Ranger or Rogue level taken later also asks for one skill.

## 5. Rebuild a character made by hand

A PC you built by hand (or imported) has **Level & CR** on its Stats tab instead. Under its classes, **Rebuild with the builder…** turns it into a built character at the level it is. The builder opens on **Review**:

![Rebuilding a hand-made Barbarian: what it keeps, and its look-alike abilities](img/build-a-character/12-rebuild.png)

- Its classes are found by name (and a subclass, when the builder has one by that name). Change the class in **Class** if it guessed wrong.
- Its **ability scores and hit point maximum stay as they are**: the builder works out the base scores that give them.
- Nothing is added to its equipment.
- Its class features become the builder's versions.

Its own abilities stay too. **Named twice** lists the ones named like something the builder adds, so you don't end up with two of each. Tick **Remove my versions when rebuilding** to take them off, or leave them to compare and remove later. Click **Rebuild**. It's one undo step.

## 6. Make a whole party

The **Quick party** box on the Character tab makes four characters at once, at the **Level** chosen above it: a Fighter, a Cleric, a Rogue and a Wizard to start. Change any of the four.

![The Quick party box](img/build-a-character/13-quick-party.png)

Each one is quick built, named after its class, and put on its own square (and, when you're adding to a folder, filed in it). It's one undo step.

![The party on the map](img/build-a-character/14-party-on-the-map.png)

## 7. Homebrew: your own classes, subclasses, feats, backgrounds and species

**Homebrew content…**, at the bottom of the Character tab (and in the builder's step list), opens the **Homebrew** window. What you save there belongs to your account and is offered in the Character tab and the builder beside the SRD's, marked *Homebrew*. Characters built from it level up with it.

![The Homebrew window, empty](img/build-a-character/15-homebrew.png)

There are four ways to start:

- **Make a new…** a class, a subclass, a feat, a background or a species.
- **Copy an SRD entry**: the quickest start for a variant. It keeps every feature, and you change what's different.
- **Import…** a file someone exported (**Export** saves the open entry as a file, **Export all** saves everything).
- **Import from Open5e…** searches Open5e's classes and subclasses (other publishers' open content). One comes in with its hit die, proficiencies, table and features by level, as reference text for you to make runnable.

### A subclass on an SRD class

A subclass names its **Class**, and that can be an SRD class: an Arcane Trickster on the Rogue, a Path of the Zealot on the Barbarian. It can have its own spellcasting (the Arcane Trickster is a third caster: Intelligence, the wizard's list) and its own table columns (the Zealot's healing dice).

![The Path of the Zealot: a subclass of the SRD Barbarian, with a table column of its own](img/build-a-character/16-subclass.png)

### Features by level

**Features by level** lists levels 1 to 20. Open one to see its features and choices. **+ Feature** makes a new feature in the ability editor, the same one the sheet uses, so anything you can make on a sheet you can make for a class. Each feature's details hold what makes it a class feature:

![A feature's details: the Spiritfire Gun's volley follows the Shots column](img/build-a-character/17-grant.png)

- **Numbers that follow the table**: pick a number or dice in the feature (here, how many shots the volley fires) and what it becomes. The buttons put in a column (`{col:shots}`), the class level (`{level}`) or the proficiency bonus (`{pb}`). Sneak Attack's dice follow its column the same way.
- **A pool of uses**: how many times it can be used (a number, or a column).
- **Replaces**: an earlier feature this one takes the place of (a better version at a higher level).
- **Speed +**, **Max HP +**, **Darkvision**, saving throw proficiencies, spells always prepared, and spells it casts without a slot.

**Edit** opens the feature in the ability editor, previewed on the class built to that level:

![The Spiritfire Gun in the ability editor](img/build-a-character/18-feature-in-editor.png)

**Done** takes it back to the class. **Check** builds the class at every level and lists anything that doesn't work out (a number that no longer exists in its feature, a missing class). **Save** stores it to your account.

![A copy of the SRD Rogue, ready to change](img/build-a-character/19-copy-srd.png)

### From Open5e

Search by name; a class's subclasses are found by its name too. The SRD classes are left out, since the builder has them already.

![Open5e's Marshal and its subclasses](img/build-a-character/20-open5e.png)

An imported class is your copy: importing it again just opens yours, so your changes are never overwritten.

> **What runs, and what doesn't.** A feature's badge says how much of it the simulator runs, as the dots on the sheet's Abilities tab and the builder's rules cards do: *Simulated*, *Partly simulated* (its text says what isn't), or *Reference only* (text for you to apply by hand). The SRD classes mark their own; a homebrew feature runs as far as you've made it run.
